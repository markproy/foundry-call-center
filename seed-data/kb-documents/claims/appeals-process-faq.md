# Filing a Claim Appeal — FAQ

## How do I appeal a denied claim?
A member (or their provider, on the member's behalf) can file a first-level
appeal in writing within the appeal deadline shown on the claim. The appeal
should include: the claim ID, the reason the member believes the denial was
incorrect, and any supporting documentation (medical records, a letter of
medical necessity from the treating provider, or proof of prior
authorization if one was in fact obtained).

## How long does an appeal take?
First-level appeals are typically reviewed within 30 calendar days of
receipt for standard requests, or 72 hours for urgent/expedited requests
(for example, when a delay would jeopardize the member's health).

## What happens if the appeal is denied?
The member has the right to request a second-level appeal, and after that,
an external independent review, depending on their plan type. Each level of
appeal has its own filing deadline, shown in the denial letter for that
level.

## Can a member appeal more than one claim at a time?
Yes — each claim is appealed separately, but if multiple claims were denied
for the same underlying reason (for example, the same missing
authorization), the member can reference the related claim IDs in a single
appeal letter to avoid duplicating paperwork.

## Does filing an appeal pause the member's payment responsibility?
No — the member's stated responsibility on the EOB remains in effect while
an appeal is pending, unless the plan explicitly places the balance on hold
during review. Members should not assume a bill can be ignored just because
an appeal was filed.
