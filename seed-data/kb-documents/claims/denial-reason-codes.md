# Claim Denial Reason Code Glossary

This glossary explains the standard adjustment reason codes ("CO" codes) that
appear on a member's Explanation of Benefits (EOB) when a claim is denied or
adjusted. Use this to translate a code returned by the claims system into a
plain-language explanation for a member.

## CO-197 — Precertification/authorization absent
The service required prior authorization from the plan before it was
performed, and no authorization was on file. This is the most common reason
a claim is denied for members who saw a specialist or had an imaging study
without first getting a referral or pre-approval from their PCP or plan.
**What the member can do:** ask their provider's office to submit a
retroactive authorization request, or file an appeal (see
appeals-process-faq.md) with documentation showing the service was medically
necessary.

## CO-B7 — Provider not certified/eligible to bill for this service
This typically means the rendering provider was out-of-network for the
member's plan at the time of service, or was not credentialed for the
specific service billed. **What the member can do:** confirm the provider's
network status, and if the member believes the provider was in-network,
file an appeal with a copy of the provider's network directory listing at
the time of service.

## CO-96 — Non-covered charge
The specific service billed is excluded from coverage under the member's
current plan (for example, certain elective procedures, cosmetic services,
or services not included in the plan's covered-benefits list). **What the
member can do:** review their Summary of Benefits, or file an appeal if they
believe the service should have been covered under a specific benefit
category (e.g., the service was medically necessary rather than elective).

## General guidance
Always confirm the claim's specific denial reason code and appeal deadline
before advising a member — appeal windows are typically 90 days from the
date of the denial notice and do not extend automatically.
