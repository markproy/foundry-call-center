# Prescription Refill Policy — FAQ

## When can a member refill a prescription?
Most maintenance medications become eligible for refill once roughly 80% of
the prior fill has been used - shown on the prescription record as the
"next refill eligible date." Requesting a refill before that date is
generally not possible through the standard mail-order or retail refill
process.

## What if a member needs an early refill?
Early refills are sometimes approved for travel, a lost or damaged
prescription, or a dosage change ordered by the prescriber. These require
the pharmacy or the plan to manually review the request - a member asking
for an early refill should be told this needs pharmacy review, not
processed automatically.

## What happens when refills run out?
Once refills reach zero, the pharmacy cannot fill the prescription again
without a new prescription order from the prescribing provider. The member
should be told plainly that they'll need their provider to send a new
prescription - this isn't something the health plan or pharmacy can
override.

## Do controlled substances follow the same refill rules?
No. Controlled substances (for example, many pain medications and some
ADHD medications) typically cannot be refilled early under any
circumstances due to federal regulation, and often require a new written
prescription for every fill regardless of refills remaining on file.
