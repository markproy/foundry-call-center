# Formulary Tiers — What They Mean

## What is a formulary tier?
The formulary is the plan's list of covered drugs, organized into tiers
that determine what a member pays out of pocket. A lower tier number
generally means a lower copay.

- **Tier 1 - Preferred generic**: lowest copay, most common medications.
- **Tier 2 - Preferred brand**: moderate copay, brand-name drugs the plan
  prefers over alternatives.
- **Tier 3 - Non-preferred brand**: higher copay, brand-name drugs with a
  preferred alternative available.
- **Tier 4 - Specialty**: highest cost-share, typically injectable or
  infused medications for complex conditions, and usually requires prior
  authorization.

## Can a member request a lower-tier alternative?
Yes, but only the prescriber can change what's actually prescribed. A
member asking to "move down a tier" should be told to discuss generic or
preferred-brand alternatives with their prescriber - the plan itself
cannot substitute a different medication into an existing prescription.

## Does formulary tier affect prior authorization?
Tier and prior-authorization requirements are related but separate. Most
Tier 4 (specialty) medications require prior authorization, but some
Tier 2 and Tier 3 medications do as well, depending on the specific drug.
Always check the prescription record's own prior-authorization fields
rather than assuming from tier alone.
