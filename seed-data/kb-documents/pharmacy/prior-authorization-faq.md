# Prescription Prior Authorization — FAQ

## What is prior authorization?
Prior authorization (PA) is a review the plan requires before certain
medications are covered - usually specialty drugs, high-cost drugs, or
drugs with lower-cost alternatives on the formulary. The prescriber's
office submits clinical justification, and the plan reviews it before the
pharmacy can fill the prescription at the covered price.

## How long does a prior authorization review take?
Standard prior authorization reviews are typically completed within 3-5
business days of the plan receiving a complete request from the
prescriber's office. Expedited review (used when a delay could seriously
harm the member's health) is typically completed within 24 hours.

## What happens if prior authorization is denied?
A denied prior authorization means the plan will not cover the medication
at this time based on the information submitted. The member's prescriber
can submit additional clinical information and request reconsideration, or
the member can file a formal appeal of the coverage decision - similar in
spirit to a claim appeal, but specific to the pharmacy benefit. The member
should also be told they can pay out of pocket for the medication while a
reconsideration or appeal is pending, if they choose to.

## Who submits the prior authorization request?
The prescribing provider's office submits the request, not the member or
the pharmacy directly. A member calling about a pending prior
authorization should be told to also follow up with their provider's
office to make sure the request was actually submitted, since a stalled
PA is often waiting on paperwork from the prescriber.
